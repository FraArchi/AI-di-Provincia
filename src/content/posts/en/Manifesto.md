I was chatting with an AI. Not for work, not for a precise project — I was looking for a way to earn something by exploiting AI, like many do. And as I wrote and read, I realized a strange thing: I had been using these tools for years, almost every day, since I was a teenager. Almost a quarter of my life with AI always beside me. And yet I didn't talk about it with anyone. There was no place to do so, at least not for someone like me.


The problem is not that we don't talk about AI. The problem is how we talk about it and to whom. In Italy, if you are not passionate, if you don't work with it, if you don't have time to follow every update, you are out. And when you finally hear something on the news, it is almost always a story of fear — robots stealing jobs, algorithms controlling everything. What is missing is the voice of those who actually use it, every day, without being a Silicon Valley expert. What is missing is the perspective of those who live in a small town and still try to understand where the world is going.

Here you will find no gurus. You will find no miraculous prompts or strategies to get rich in thirty days. You will find AI seen from the province — the real one, the village one, the one that doesn't make news but exists. Articles written to understand, not to impress. A space for those who missed something along the way, because AI runs fast and nobody waited for us. This project was born for me, honestly — because writing is good for me, reasoning out loud is good for me. But if you live in a reality similar to mine, I think you will find something that belongs to you.

I don't know where this journey will lead. But I know where it starts: from an open chat on the computer, from an unanswered question, and from the desire to tell someone about it.
